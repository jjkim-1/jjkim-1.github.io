About

Welcome to my personal website, my name is James Kim. I am a recent USC graduate with an M.S. degree in Aerospace Engineering.

I did my undergraduate at UCLA, triple majoring in Biochemistry, Microbiology, Immunology, and Molecular Genetics (MIMG), and Neuroscience.

You might be wondering why I switched to Aerospace Engineering. Here are a few reasons:

  • My undergraduate background in biochemistry and other sciences gave me experience with rigourous experimental research, but I realized that I was most motivated by problems where I could personally design, build, test, and improve complete systems.
  • Engineering gave me the opportunity to work at a larger system level, where changes to individual components, models, and design parameeters can be evaluated against the performance of the overall system. Upon taking my first controls course, I discovered a strong interest in the field because of its emphasis on designing, simulating, testing, and continously improving complex systems.
  • I became interested in aerospace and defense systems because of the interaction between guidance and control, propulsion, sensing, vehicle dynamics, and other subsystems. I enjoy understanding how these components work together and are able to identify where performance parameters can be improved when a system does not behave as expected.
  • Ultimately, I want to apply my engineering background to technologies that contribute to national security, protect the United States and its allies, and help defend military personnel and civilians from emerging threats.

Pursuing three undergraduate majors taught me how to learn quickly, adapt to unfamiliar technical subjects, and approach problems from multiple perspectives. Those skills have transferred directly into my engineering background at USC where I have been able to build proficiency across subjects such as controls, flight dynamics, simulation, and combustion fundamentals. My academic background has made me comfortable stepping into new technical domains to develop the knowledge needed to succeed in the industry to ultimately solve complex engineering problems.
